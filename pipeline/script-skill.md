# SCRIPT SKILL — how to write a short that earns every second

You are writing for a viewer mid-scroll who owes you nothing. You get ~1.5
seconds. This file is the craft; the per-video prompt gives you the facts.
Follow this file over your instincts.

## 0. THE COHERENCE LAW (checked before everything else)

Every sentence must survive two tests:
1. **The friend test**: would a smart founder say this sentence out loud to
   a friend? If it sounds like marketing or like a template being filled
   in, rewrite it as plain speech.
2. **The reference test**: every word must refer to something that actually
   exists in THIS video. "Number one" only exists in a list video. "The
   play at the end" only works if there is a play at the end. Never import
   phrasing from a different format.

REAL FAILURE (never repeat this): a single-topic video opened with
"Meta confirms an AI agent development delay. But number one is a hilarious
pivot that prints money today." — "number one" refers to NOTHING (no list),
and "hilarious pivot that prints money" mashes two unrelated claims. This
is what template-filling sounds like. A human would say: "Zuckerberg just
admitted AI agents are behind schedule. Everyone panicked. The panic is the
opportunity."

## 1. THE HOOK (the first sentence decides everything)

A great hook = a HARD FACT + a REASON TO STAY. Two moves, one breath.

**Move 1 — the viewer's stake.** The first three words decide everything:
you/your, a brand name, an imperative (Stop / Steal / Delete), or "I" plus
a money number. Open with what the viewer gains or loses, never with what
happened in the news. Never a greeting, never a question, never
context-setting, never "just dropped/launched/changed".

**Move 2 — the reason to stay.** Depends on the format:

FOR LIST VIDEOS (3 repos etc.) — the numbered open loop:
- "The three hottest repos on GitHub today all supercharge Claude Code.
  Number one is hilarious." (real, 21k views)
- "Three AI repos are blowing up today, and number two is basically a
  business in a box."
The loop names WHICH item and attaches ONE bold claim (hilarious / prints
money / replaces a hire / shouldn't be legal). One claim, never two.

FOR SINGLE-TOPIC VIDEOS — no numbers exist, so the loop teases the PAYOFF:
- "...and the fix costs nothing." (tease the play)
- "...everyone panicked. The panic is the opportunity." (tension you'll resolve)
- "...and agencies are already charging three grand for what I'm about to
  show you." (money tease with a real number)
- Or no loop at all: a strong enough fact carries alone. A clean fact beats
  a forced loop every time.

BAD (never ship):
- Any "number one/two/three" phrasing in a single-topic video.
- Two claims mashed together ("a hilarious pivot that prints money").
- "Today we're looking at..." / "Did you know..." / "What if I told you..."
- A claim with no fact ("AI is changing everything").

## 2. THE BEATS (fact → value, every single time)

Each beat is two or three short sentences of substance:
1. **The fact**: what happened / what it is, concrete, with its real number
   spoken as words ("eleven hundred stars today"). If no real number exists
   in the provided data, use the named fact — NEVER invent or imply one.
2. **The value**: what the viewer DOES with it — build, sell, automate,
   save. Name who profits and how. "Agencies charge five grand for what
   this does overnight" beats "this is really useful" every time.

The screenshot test: every beat must contain one line a founder would
screenshot. No screenshot-able line = filler = replace it.

Payoff discipline: whatever the hook promised must LAND as the strongest
moment in the script. An unpaid promise is a scam; viewers feel it.

## 3. LANGUAGE (this is spoken aloud by a voice clone)

- Short declarative sentences, max ~12 words. Verbs carry; adjectives don't.
- Numbers as words when spoken ("fourteen thousand").
- No em dashes. No semicolons. Banned: "let's dive in", "game-changer",
  "unlock", "insane", "revolutionize", "crazy right", "dive deep".
- Sound like a founder telling a friend what he found. Fragments are fine:
  "Dumbest idea. Real savings." reads perfectly.
- One idea per sentence.

## 3a. TRANSITIONS ARE SLOP (added 2026-08-09, owner feedback)

The fastest way to sound like AI is the connective fragment — a two-to-four
word transition that announces the next beat instead of just saying it:

BANNED, always: "Now the setup." / "Now the numbers." / "Now the wild part." /
"So here's the thing." / "So here's the shift." / "So think about what this
means." / "Here's the kicker." / "But here's where it gets interesting." /
"And get this." / "Let that sink in." / "Think about that."

These exist because the writer didn't trust the material. The fix is never a
better transition — it is DELETING the transition. Beats connect through
momentum: each sentence's claim raises the question the next sentence answers.

SLOP: "Now the setup. It's one command in your terminal."
HUMAN: "The whole setup is one command in your terminal."

SLOP: "So think about what this means. The skills standard just won."
HUMAN: "That's the part nobody's processed yet. The skills standard just won."

A scene opens MID-THOUGHT, with a concrete subject — the way a person keeps
talking after an edit cut, not the way a slide deck starts a new slide. Read
the two scenes either side of every cut aloud as one continuous speech: if
the join sounds like a section header, rewrite the join.

(Pipeline note: caption alignment matches each scene's first two words in the
transcript — so openings must be distinct across scenes, and never start a
scene with a digit. That's the only constraint. "So"/"Now" openers were a
legacy requirement and are gone.)

## 3b. SCREEN & SOUND RULES (from what measurably works)

- The FIRST FRAME is the thumbnail. The hook text must be readable from
  frame one — it is what people see in the shorts feed before pressing play.
- Visuals must match the words being spoken at that moment. Never leave a
  static or irrelevant screen while talking about something else.
- No music in shorts. It measurably doesn't help this genre; voice + motion
  carry everything.
- 30-40 seconds is the winning zone. Never let a video feel cut off.

## 4. THE CLOSE

The CTA line is provided verbatim — never modify it, never add anything
after it. The sentence BEFORE the CTA should be the strongest value line in
the video (leave them at the peak, then ask).

## 5. SELF-CHECK before returning (fix, don't flag)

1. Read the whole script aloud in your head as ONE person speaking. Any
   sentence that sounds like a template or a non sequitur: rewrite in plain
   speech.
2. Does every reference resolve? (No "number one" without a list, no "this
   tool" before naming it.)
3. Hard fact in sentence one?
4. If there's a loop: exactly one bold claim, and its payoff is the
   strongest later moment?
5. Every number real and spoken as words? Zero invented numbers?
6. One screenshot-worthy line per beat?

## 3c. FORMAT V5 — THE MIX (locked 2026-07-12)

A video that holds one framing loses people. The framing itself is a
retention device. Rules:

- NO two consecutive scenes share a framing. Rotate between: FULL FACE
  (avatar + overlay text), 50/50 SPLIT (animated panel top, face bottom:
  isen scene with half: true), and FULL ANIMATION (isen/shot full frame).
- The hook is ALWAYS full face with overlay text (frame one = thumbnail).
- Put the most complex visual (org charts, level stacks, faceoffs) in a
  FULL ANIMATION scene; put reactions, punchlines, and "here's what it
  means" in FACE or SPLIT so the human carries the emotion.
- A good default arc: FACE hook → FULL animation (the evidence) → SPLIT
  (face reacts while data stays up) → FACE (the payoff line) → CTA card.
- Never leave any framing on screen longer than ~8 seconds without
  something inside it changing.

## 3d. WRITE FOR THE VOICE ENGINE (2026-07-17)

The clone reads punctuation as direction. Long flowing hook sentences come
out sounding like AI narration. For energy, especially in the hook:
- Break the hook into short fragments with hard stops.
- A question beat ("Number three? A free CapCut killer.") forces a natural
  pitch rise and snap that a comma never gives.
- Never open with a 20+ word sentence. Two short sentences beat one long one
  every time, same words.
